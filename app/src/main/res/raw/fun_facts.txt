# Showdist fun facts (SPEC.md §1.4). One fact per block, blocks separated by a blank line.
# An optional second line "source: NAME" adds a small citation under the fact; use one for
# anything surprising or hard to believe. Lines starting with # are ignored.
# Every fact here was checked against a reliable source before being added.

In 1791 France defined the metre as one ten-millionth of the distance from the North Pole to the equator, along the meridian through Paris.
source: BIPM

Since 1983 the metre has been the distance light travels in a vacuum in 1/299,792,458 of a second.
source: BIPM

The speed of light is now fixed by definition, so the length of a metre depends on how precisely we measure time.
source: BIPM

Since 1959 the inch has been exactly 25.4 mm and the foot exactly 0.3048 m, so US customary units are legally defined in metric terms.
source: NIST

The US survey foot, two parts per million longer than the international foot, was retired at the end of 2022.
source: NIST

The CIA World Factbook lists only three countries as not officially metric: the United States, Liberia and Myanmar. Liberia and Myanmar have both pledged to switch.
source: CIA World Factbook

Many old units came from the body: the foot from a foot, the inch from the width of a thumb.

The Egyptian cubit was the length from elbow to the tip of the middle finger; surviving royal cubit rods measure about 52 cm.
source: Britannica

In 1670 Gabriel Mouton, a French priest, proposed a decimal system of length based on the size of the Earth.
source: MacTutor, St Andrews

Melchisédech Thévenot built the first known spirit level some time before 1661, as his letters to Christiaan Huygens show.
source: Dictionary of Scientific Biography

A spirit level's vial is very slightly curved, so the bubble always settles at the highest point.

Spirit levels are filled with alcohol, the "spirit", because it flows freely and won't freeze in the cold.

In 1999 the Mars Climate Orbiter was lost when one team used pound-force seconds and another newton-seconds.
source: NASA

Around 240 BC Eratosthenes estimated Earth's circumference by comparing the noon Sun's angle at Syene and Alexandria.
source: Britannica

The nautical mile began as one minute of latitude. Since 1929 it has been exactly 1,852 metres, and sailors and pilots still use it.
source: International Hydrographic Organization

In 2019 the kilogram was redefined by the Planck constant. It was the last base unit still tied to a physical object, a metal cylinder kept near Paris since 1889.
source: BIPM

Revolutionary France briefly tried decimal time: 10 hours a day, 100 minutes an hour. It was compulsory for only about six months, in 1794 and 1795.
source: Britannica

Edmund Gunter's 1620 surveying chain is 66 feet long. 80 chains make a mile and 10 square chains an acre, which is why an acre is 43,560 square feet.
source: NIST

"Inch" comes from the Latin uncia, one twelfth, the same word that gave us "ounce".
source: Merriam-Webster

According to legend, the yard was the distance from King Henry I's nose to the tip of his thumb.

A fathom is six feet, roughly the span of a person's outstretched arms.

The mile comes from the Roman mille passus: a thousand paces, each pace two steps.

The furlong, 220 yards, takes its name from the length of a furrow in a ploughed field.

One degree off over a ten-foot shelf puts the far end about two inches out.

A one-degree slope is about a 1.75 percent grade, so a bubble that looks barely off-centre can still mean a visibly crooked picture.

Phones sense tilt with an accelerometer that feels gravity: the digital cousin of the bubble in a vial.

Self-levelling laser levels hang their optics on a tiny pendulum, so gravity keeps the beam true: the bubble's idea, automated.

Egyptologists Mark Lehner and I. E. S. Edwards suggested the Giza builders levelled the site with water-filled trenches, since water finds its own level.
source: Mark Lehner, The Complete Pyramids

A plumb bob, a weight on a string, is one of the oldest building tools and still in use, because gravity never needs calibrating.

Apollo 11, 14 and 15 left mirrors on the Moon; lasers bounced off them measure its distance to the millimetre.
source: NASA

Those lunar lasers show the Moon drifting about 3.8 cm farther from Earth every year.
source: NASA

Sunlight takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to reach Earth.
source: NASA

Since 2012 the astronomical unit has been exactly 149,597,870,700 metres.
source: IAU

From 1889 to 1960 the metre was the length of a platinum-iridium bar kept near Paris.
source: BIPM

In 1960 the metre was redefined as 1,650,763.73 wavelengths of light from krypton-86.
source: BIPM

In 1615 Willebrord Snellius began measuring the Earth by triangulation across the Netherlands.
source: Britannica

Light travels about 30 cm in a nanosecond; Grace Hopper handed out wires that long to show it.
source: Smithsonian

A light-year is about 9.46 trillion kilometres; a parsec is about 3.26 light-years.
source: IAU

In 2020 China and Nepal jointly measured Mount Everest at 8,848.86 metres.
source: Survey Department of Nepal

# Everyday formulas (SPEC.md §1.4, §1.8): short tips; common knowledge, so no sources.

Handy formula: a rectangle's area is length × width, a triangle's is half the base × the height, and a circle's is π × the radius squared.

A circle's circumference is 2 × π × its radius, and π is roughly 3.14.

A box's volume is length × width × height.

The Pythagorean theorem: in a right triangle, a² + b² = c², where c is the long side.

The 3-4-5 trick: if two sides measure 3 and 4 and the diagonal between their ends measures 5, the corner is perfectly square.

For 10 percent, move the decimal point one place left. A 15 percent tip is 10 percent plus half of that.

Percent change is the difference divided by the original amount, times 100.

One inch is exactly 2.54 centimetres; a kilometre is about 0.62 of a mile, and a kilogram about 2.2 pounds.

Celsius to Fahrenheit: multiply by 1.8 and add 32, or for a quick guess, double it and add 30.

Speed is distance divided by time: 120 miles in 2 hours is 60 miles an hour.
